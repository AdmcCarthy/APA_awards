The shelf

56 new licences, 24,000 km²

Every year the government opens a predefined area of mature acreage for applications (shown in pale blue). The new licences, coloured by operator, sit among the hundreds that already exist (grey).

North Sea · 30 licences · 7,595 km²

Small pieces next to big hubs

A typical North Sea licence is just 80 km². Ten are stratigraphic, cut to fit above or below licences that already exist. The outlier is DNO's 1278, which covers 2,698 km² in the far south near Trym.

Norwegian Sea · 21 licences · 11,356 km²

Where the acreage went

The Norwegian Sea took 47% of the area, mostly in four licences more than 60 km from any producing field. Three are just north of 62°N; Aker BP's 1308 is far out on the Vøring margin.

Barents Sea · 5 licences · 5,011 km²

One licence, one-fifth of the round

Aker BP's 1313 covers 4,345 km² and lies 106 km from Snøhvit. It is the largest and most remote licence in the round, with 18% of all the acreage awarded.

Who operates

Two companies run half the licences

Equinor operates 16 and Aker BP 12. Counting partner stakes too, Equinor is in 35 of the 56.

1 · The winners

Equinor wins on count, Aker BP on area

Weight each licence by the company's equity share and the ranking changes. Aker BP ends up with about 9,150 km² net, far ahead of Equinor's 5,600 km², because it took large stakes in the big Barents and Norwegian Sea blocks.

Petoro holds the state's direct financial interest (SDFI) and never operates. The state took a 10–40% share in 20 of the licences.

2 · Near-field

Most licences are within sight of a platform

47 of 56 licences lie within 25 km of a producing or approved field. Half are within 2.1 km.

The few remote licences are also the largest. The six more than 50 km out hold 48% of the awarded area.

3 · Two decades of APA

Most licences are handed back

The predefined area has grown from 84,700 km² in 2003 to 458,100 km² in 2025. Most of what gets awarded is returned. Of the 610 licences from rounds up to 2015, only 12% are still active. At that rate, six or seven of this year's 56 would still be active ten years from now.

4 · Partnerships

Who shares licences with whom

Equinor shares at least one licence with every other company in the matrix. Its closest partners are Petoro (13) and Vår Energi (11). Aker BP's are DNO and Equinor (9 each). Some smaller partners stick close to one company: Aker BP is in all five of ORLEN's licences.

5 · Since the award

Four licences have already changed hands

Partners in APA licences often farm in, farm out or swap stakes within months of the award. Between June and October 2026, Sodir registered new ownership for four of the 56 licences.

Methods and caveats

Data

  • All layers come from Sodir FactMaps, retrieved 6 November 2026: licences, licensee history, blocks, fields, the predefined area and the basemap. Sodir codes APA rounds TFO<year>; this page covers TFO2025.
  • The ministry announced 57 offers on 13 January 2026, but Sodir lists 56 licences, all granted on 20 March 2026. Because of this, counts here can be one lower than in the press release. For example, Equinor operates 16 licences here and 17 in the release.
  • Partners and shares are the line-up on the grant date. Changes after that appear only in section 5.
  • Areas are Sodir's area at award, in km². Net acreage is area × equity share.

Geometry and colour

  • Sodir's native datum is ED50; geometries were requested in WGS84. The shift of about 100 m is below the resolution of these maps.
  • Distances are edge to edge in ETRS89-LAEA (EPSG:3035), to fields that are "Producing" or "Approved for production".
  • The map uses a Lambert conformal conic projection (58° and 72° N, central meridian 14° E). Outlines are simplified to about 300 m.
  • Operator colours start from each company's brand hue. Lightness and saturation were then adjusted until every pair stays distinct under simulated colour-vision deficiency, in both themes. They approximate the brands and are not official brand values.
