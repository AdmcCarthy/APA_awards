The shelf

458,000 km² on offer, 24,000 km² taken

The pale blue wash is the APA 2025 predefined area, the mature acreage where companies may apply every year. Grey shapes are licences that already exist. The coloured shapes are the 56 new licences, coloured by operator.

Seen from this far out, the new acreage almost disappears into the existing licence map. That's intended: APA is meant to keep exploration going around infrastructure that is already in place.

North Sea · 30 licences · 7,595 km²

Infill around the big hubs

More than half of the licences are in the North Sea, but they are small: a median of 80 km². Equinor's cluster runs from Sleipner north to Troll and Gullfaks. Ten of the North Sea awards are stratigraphic, granted only above or below a given level, so they slot under or over licences that already exist.

The exception is DNO's licence 1278. At 2,698 km² it is the largest in the North Sea, and it lies in the far south near Trym, close to the Danish median line.

Norwegian Sea · 21 licences · 11,356 km²

The area winner

The Norwegian Sea took 47% of the acreage. Four large licences each lie more than 60 km from the nearest producing field. Equinor's 1297, Aker BP's 1296 and Vår Energi's 1298 sit just north of 62°N, south-west of Ormen Lange. Aker BP's 1308 lies far out on the western Vøring margin.

Closer in, Aker BP, Harbour and OKEA picked up small pieces next to Skarv, Alve and Dvalin, the sort of near-field tiebacks this round was designed for.

Barents Sea · 5 licences · 5,011 km²

One licence, one-fifth of the round

Aker BP's licence 1313 covers 4,345 km², 18% of everything awarded and almost nine-tenths of the Barents Sea total. At 106 km from Snøhvit, it is also the most remote licence in the round.

The other four Barents awards sit close to Goliat, Snøhvit and Johan Castberg.

Who operates

Two companies operate half the licences

Equinor operates 16 licences and Aker BP 12, so between them they run 28 of the 56. Equinor is a partner in 35 licences in all, nearly two-thirds of the round. The colours follow each company's brand hue, adjusted so that readers with colour-vision deficiency can tell them apart. Grey covers the seven smaller operators.

Chapter 1 · The winners

Equinor wins on licence count, Aker BP on acreage

Licence counts flatter companies that take small slivers next to their own fields. Weighting each licence by the company's equity share gives a different ranking. Aker BP's net acreage, about 9,150 km², is well ahead of Equinor's 5,600 km², because Aker BP went big in the Barents and Norwegian seas.

Petoro manages the State's Direct Financial Interest (SDFI) and never operates. The state took a share in 20 of the 56 licences, at between 10% and 40%.

Chapter 2 · Near-field

Most of the new licences are within sight of a platform

47 of the 56 licences lie within 25 km of a producing or approved field. Half are within 2.1 km.

The few licences far from infrastructure are also the biggest. The six licences more than 50 km from a field hold 51% of the awarded area.

Chapter 3 · Two decades of APA

The predefined area has grown fivefold, and most licences are handed back

APA started in 2003 with 84,700 km² of predefined area. Each year the ministry adds blocks to it. The APA 2025 area was 458,100 km², and APA 2026 has grown it again to 483,900 km².

Most APA licences are short-lived. Of the 850 licences awarded in the rounds up to 2015, only 11% are still active. The rest have been relinquished, usually after a dry well or a decision not to drill. If that rate holds, six or seven of this year's 56 will still be active a decade from now.

Chapter 4 · Partnerships

Who applied together

The ministry puts the licence groups together, but they mostly follow the companies' own applications. Equinor and Petoro share 13 licences. Equinor and Vår Energi share 11, and Aker BP pairs with DNO and with Equinor on nine each.

Chapter 5 · Since the ink dried

Four licences have already changed hands

Licence groups in APA rounds are often reshuffled through farm-ins and swaps within months of the award. Sodir's register shows new licensee line-ups on four of the 56 licences between June and October 2026.

Methods and caveats

Data

  • Licences, licensees, blocks, quadrants, fields, the predefined-area outline and the basemap (land, maritime boundaries, towns) all come from the Sodir FactMaps REST services, retrieved 6 November 2026. Sodir labels APA rounds TFO<year>, and this page covers TFO2025.
  • The ministry announced 57 offers on 13 January 2026. Sodir's register lists 56 licences under TFO2025, all granted 20 March 2026. I could not determine from the register why one is missing, so licence and operator counts here (Equinor 35/16, DNO 16/4, ORLEN 5/0) differ by one from the ministry press release (35/17, 17/4, 6/0).
  • Partners and equity shares are the line-up on the grant date. Later transfers are shown in Chapter 5 and not used elsewhere.
  • Licence areas are Sodir's area at award, in km². Net acreage is area × equity share.

Geometry

  • Sodir's native datum is ED50 (EPSG:4230). Geometries were requested in WGS84; the shift of about 100 m is below the resolution of these maps.
  • Distances use ETRS89-LAEA (EPSG:3035), measured edge to edge to the nearest field with status "Producing" or "Approved for production". Stratigraphic licences touching a field come out at 0 km, even when they are vertically separated from the field's reservoir.
  • The map uses a Lambert conformal conic projection (standard parallels 58° and 72° N, central meridian 14° E). Outlines were simplified to around 300 m.

    • Colour

  • Operator colours start from each company's brand hue: Equinor red, Aker BP blue, Vår Energi green, DNO amber, Harbour light blue, Wellesley violet. They are then shifted in lightness and chroma until every pair separates under protanopia and deuteranopia simulation, in both light and dark themes. They are approximations, not official brand values. Smaller operators share one grey and are identified by tooltip and table.
