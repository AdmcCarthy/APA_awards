The shelf

56 new licences among hundreds of old ones

Every year the government opens a predefined area of mature acreage for applications (shown in pale blue). The new licences, coloured by operator, almost vanish among the existing ones (grey). That is by design: APA exists to keep exploration going around infrastructure that is already in place.

North Sea · 30 licences

Slices around the hubs

Half of the North Sea licences are within 1.7 km of a producing field. Ten are stratigraphic, granted only above or below a set depth so they stack onto existing licences. Eleven more extend licences that already exist.

Norwegian Sea · 21 licences

Tiebacks, and a few long shots

Aker BP, Vår Energi and Harbour took acreage touching Alve, Kristin and Skarv. Further out, four licences lie more than 60 km from the nearest field. They are the round's main step-outs, and Aker BP operates two of them.

Barents Sea · 5 licences

Going it alone

Aker BP's 1313 lies 106 km from Snøhvit, the most remote licence in the round, and Aker BP holds it with no partners. Only one other licence has a single owner: Aker BP's 1308. The other Barents awards sit close to Goliat and Snøhvit.

Who operates

Two companies run half the licences

Equinor operates 16 and Aker BP 12. Counting partner stakes too, Equinor is in 35 of the 56.

1 · The winners

Equinor is almost everywhere

Equinor operates 16 licences and is a partner in 19 more, so it is in nearly two of every three. Aker BP operates 12. Further down, the strategies differ: Wellesley operates every licence it holds, while ORLEN takes partner stakes only.

2 · Near-field

Each operator's distance from home

21 of 56 licences lie within 1 km of a producing field. 47 are within 25 km.

Harbour is the purest near-field player: all four licences it operates are stratigraphic, and three touch or nearly touch a producing field. Aker BP is the opposite. It takes small extensions next to Alve and Ivar Aasen, and also operates three of the six licences more than 50 km from any field.

3 · Two decades of APA

Most licences are handed back

Most APA licences are given back. Of the 610 licences from rounds up to 2015, only 12% are still active. At that rate, six or seven of this year's 56 would still be active ten years from now.

The mix of seas barely moves from year to year. Interest in the Barents Sea peaked in APA 2018 and 2019, with 14 and 13 licences, and has not come back.

4 · Partnerships

Who shares licences with whom

Equinor shares at least one licence with every other company in the matrix. Its closest partners are Petoro (13) and Vår Energi (11). Aker BP's are DNO and Equinor (9 each). Some smaller partners stick close to one company: Aker BP is in all five of ORLEN's licences.

5 · Since the award

Four licences have already changed hands

Partners in APA licences often farm in, farm out or swap stakes within months of the award. Between June and October 2026, Sodir registered new ownership for four of the 56 licences.

Methods and caveats

Data

  • All layers come from Sodir FactMaps, retrieved 6 November 2026: licences, licensee history, blocks, fields, the predefined area and the basemap. Sodir codes APA rounds TFO<year>; this page covers TFO2025.
  • The ministry announced 57 offers on 13 January 2026, but Sodir lists 56 licences, all granted on 20 March 2026. Because of this, counts here can be one lower than in the press release. For example, Equinor operates 16 licences here and 17 in the release.
  • Partners and shares are the line-up on the grant date. Changes after that appear only in section 5.
  • Areas are Sodir's area at award, in km². Net acreage is area × equity share.

Geometry and colour

  • Sodir's native datum is ED50; geometries were requested in WGS84. The shift of about 100 m is below the resolution of these maps.
  • Distances are edge to edge in ETRS89-LAEA (EPSG:3035), to fields that are "Producing" or "Approved for production".
  • The map uses a Lambert conformal conic projection (58° and 72° N, central meridian 14° E). Outlines are simplified to about 300 m.
  • Operator colours start from each company's brand hue and are deliberately muted. Lightness and saturation were tuned so that every pair stays distinct under simulated colour-vision deficiency, in both themes. They approximate the brands and are not official brand values.
